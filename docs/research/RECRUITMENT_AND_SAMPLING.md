## 1. Sampling Strategy & Target Population

To ensure empirical validity, the qualitative research sample must represent genuine diversity across study habits, academic disciplines, and campus living arrangements.

### 1.1 Core Cohort Criteria (5–7 University Students)
The primary qualitative sample consists strictly of enrolled university students who engage in coursework requiring individual study or peer collaboration.

| Sampling Dimension | Diversity Target | Target Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Academic Discipline** | Minimum 3 STEM (e.g., Computer Science, Engineering, Mathematics) & Minimum 2 Non-STEM (e.g., Humanities, Business, Social Sciences). | Problem-solving dynamics in quantitative fields differ significantly from discussion-heavy conceptual fields. |
| **Living / Commute Context** | Mix of Campus Residential Students (living in dorms/campus housing) and Commuter Students (traveling &ge; 30 mins to campus). | Commuters face severe logistical constraints and fragmented time windows compared to residential students. |
| **Group Study Experience** | Mix of Frequent Group Users (&ge; 3 hrs/week), Occasional Users (exam prep only), and Novice/Reluctant Users (struggled to form groups). | Uncovers both effective workarounds and the friction points that prevent hesitant learners from participating. |
| **Academic Level** | Mix of Lower-Division (1st/2nd year) and Upper-Division/Graduate students. | Early-stage students face higher social friction; senior students experience more specialized syllabus demands. |

### 1.2 Supplementary Cohort: Independent Learners
* Autonomous self-paced learners (e.g., individuals taking online bootcamps, professional certifications, or self-directed programming courses) may be interviewed as a **supplementary group** (`P-SUP-01`, etc.).
* **Mandatory Rule:** Supplementary independent learners **must not replace** the required 5–7 core university student participants.

## 4. Recruitment Outreach Scripts

### 4.1 Campus Discord / Community Channel Outreach Message
> **Subject:** Research Study: Improving How Students Form & Run Study Groups (25–35 min interview)
>
> Hi everyone! I am conducting an open-source research study on how students discover, organize, and sustain peer study groups. Whether you study in large groups every week, prefer silent co-study, or have struggled to find reliable study partners, your experience is invaluable.
>
> We are hosting 25–35 minute one-on-one interviews (in-person on campus or via audio call). All feedback is 100% anonymized, strictly voluntary, and used to inform an open-source collaborative learning platform called CohortCircle.
>
> If you are interested in sharing your experiences, please reply directly or fill out our quick 2-minute screener form: [Link to Screener]. Thank you!

### 4.2 In-Person Campus Library / Lounge Approach Script
> *"Hello! Excuse me—I'm conducting a quick research study on how university students organize study groups and manage their study routines for an open-source project called CohortCircle. We are conducting short 25–35 minute confidential interviews. We never collect sensitive personal information, and everything is completely anonymized. Would you be open to sharing your study experiences with us this week?"*
