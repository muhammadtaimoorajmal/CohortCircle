# Semi-Structured Interview Guide: CohortCircle

> **Method:** Qualitative Semi-Structured In-Depth Interview  
> **Target Sample:** 5 to 7 University Students (`P-01` to `P-07`)  
> **Source Identifiers:** `INT-01` through `INT-07`  
> **Session Duration:** 25–35 Minutes  
> **Status:** **INSTRUMENT PREPARED — FIELDWORK PENDING**

---

## 1. Interviewer Instructions & Ground Rules

1. **Non-Leading Stance:** Avoid suggesting answers, finishing participants' sentences, or pitching CohortCircle solutions. Listen 80% of the time, speak 20%.
2. **Neutral Validation:** Validate their feelings without agreeing on software features (e.g., say *"I see, that sounds challenging"* rather than *"Yes, our app solves that"*).
3. **Pacing & Time Management:**
   * Phase 1 (Warm-Up & Learning Context): ~5 minutes
   * Phase 2 (Group Formation & Breakdown Experiences): ~10 minutes
   * Phase 3 (Scheduling & Coordination Workflows): ~10 minutes
   * Phase 4 (Ideal Peer Learning & Wrap-Up): ~5–10 minutes
4. **Probe for Real Stories:** When participants offer abstract generalizations (e.g., *"People are flaky"*), probe for concrete recent instances: *"Can you tell me about the most recent time that happened?"*

## 3. Four-Phase Semi-Structured Interview Script

### Opening Script & Consent Check (2 minutes)
> *"Hello! Thank you so much for taking the time to speak with me today. My name is [Interviewer Name], and I am working on an open-source project called CohortCircle, which focuses on understanding how learners form, manage, and coordinate study groups.*
>
> *Before we begin, I want to reassure you that this session is completely confidential. We will never share your name or personal details, and everything will be represented under an anonymous ID code. Your participation is 100% voluntary, and if there is any question you'd prefer to skip or if you'd like to stop at any time, just let me know.*
>
> *[If recording opted-in]: As confirmed on your consent form, I will be recording the audio solely for my notes. May I start the recording now? Great, thank you!"*

---

### Phase 1: Warm-Up & Learning Context (~5 mins)
*Objective: Build rapport and understand the participant's current academic landscape and solitary vs. collaborative baseline.*

* **Q01:** "To start off, could you briefly share what subjects or topics you are currently focusing on, and how you typically study for them on a day-to-day basis?"
  * *Probe A:* "Which subjects do you find require the most time or effort?"
  * *Probe B:* "Do you study mostly on campus, at home, or elsewhere?"
* **Q02:** "Do you usually study alone, in pairs, or in larger groups? What drives that preference for you?"
  * *Probe A:* "What kinds of tasks do you feel are best done completely alone?"
  * *Probe B:* "What kinds of tasks make you want to reach out to classmates or peers?"

---

### Phase 2: Past Group Formation Experiences (~10 mins)
*Objective: Explore the organic mechanics, social barriers, and failure modes of study group creation.*

* **Q03:** "Think back to the last time you tried to form or join a study group. Walk me through how that group actually came together from the very beginning."
  * *Probe A:* "Who initiated it, and what channel was used (e.g., in-class conversation, WhatsApp, Discord, forum)?"
  * *Probe B:* "Did you know everyone beforehand, or were there people you had never met?"
* **Q04:** "What worked well during that group formation process, and where did you experience friction, awkwardness, or hesitation?"
  * *Probe A:* "How did you feel about reaching out to peers you didn't know well?"
  * *Probe B:* "How did you know whether your academic goals or expectations aligned?"
* **Q05:** "Have you ever joined a study group that didn't meet your expectations or fell apart after just one or two sessions? What happened?"
  * *Probe A:* "What was the main reason the group stopped meeting?"
  * *Probe B:* "Was there any discussion before it dissolved, or did it just fade away silently?"

---

### Phase 3: Scheduling, Coordination & Study Habits (~10 mins)
*Objective: Deep-dive into temporal friction, differing study habits, and communication channel fragmentation.*

* **Q06:** "How do you and your study partners typically decide when, where, and how long to meet?"
  * *Probe A:* "How many back-and-forth messages or polls does it usually take to pick a time?"
  * *Probe B:* "What happens when someone has to cancel or reschedule at the last minute?"
* **Q07:** "How do you handle differences in study habits—for example, if one person wants quiet focus and another wants to talk through every problem out loud?"
  * *Probe A:* "Have you ever experienced conflict or frustration over mismatched study paces?"
  * *Probe B:* "Do your study groups usually establish an agenda or ground rules, or is it spontaneous?"
* **Q08:** "What tools do you currently use to coordinate your group study (e.g., chat apps, calendars, shared folders), and what frustrates you most about them?"
  * *Probe A:* "What information gets lost or buried in chat threads?"
  * *Probe B:* "Do group chats ever become distracting or veer off into casual socialization?"

---

### Phase 4: Envisioning Ideal Peer Learning & Wrap-Up (~5–10 mins)
*Objective: Uncover unmet needs, trust factors, and mental models for compatibility matching.*

* **Q09:** "If you could design an ideal way to connect with study partners who match your exact pace, schedule, and study style, what would that look like?"
  * *Probe A:* "What would make you feel comfortable reaching out to a stranger or joining an open circle?"
  * *Probe B:* "Would you prefer open groups where anyone can join, or groups where a host reviews requests first?"
* **Q10:** "What specific information about a study group would you absolutely need to see before deciding whether to commit your time to them?"
  * *Probe A:* "How important is knowing their specific study format (e.g., silent co-study vs. problem solving)?"
  * *Probe B:* "What would make you trust that a suggested group is actually a good match for you?"

---

### Closing Script
> *"That covers all of my questions! Is there anything else about your study habits, group experiences, or frustrations that we haven't touched on today?*
>
> *Thank you again so much for your time and honest insights. Your input will directly shape our design decisions. Have a wonderful rest of your day!"*
