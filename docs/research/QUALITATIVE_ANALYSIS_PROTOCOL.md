# Qualitative Analysis Protocol & Coding Framework: CohortCircle

> **Methodology:** Systematic Thematic Analysis (Braun & Clarke Framework) & Evidence Triangulation  
> **Status:** **FRAMEWORK INSTRUMENTED — THEMATIC CODING PENDING FIELDWORK**  
> **Integrity Rule:** Candidate codes are analytical guides, NOT discovered themes. Empirical themes and validated user needs (`NEED-XXX`) will be synthesized only after genuine fieldwork records are collected.

---

## 1. Qualitative Research Architecture & Epistemology

CohortCircle applies an inductive-deductive hybrid thematic analysis approach:
* **Deductive Anchoring:** Guided by pre-established research inquiries (`RQ-01` through `RQ-08`) and the working problem hypothesis (`HYP-01`).
* **Inductive Openness:** Open to unexpected user behaviors, novel workarounds, emotional pain points, and contradictory findings discovered during naturalistic observations and open-ended interviews.

---

## 2. Nine-Step Analysis Workflow

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      9-Step Thematic Analysis Pipeline                 │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
 [Step 1: Data Familiarization] ────┴───> [Step 2: Systematic Open Coding]
                                                        │
 [Step 4: Candidate Theme Formulation] <─ [Step 3: Pattern Identification]
                │
                └───> [Step 5: Cross-Participant Matrix Analysis]
                                        │
 [Step 7: Triangulation Synthesis] <────┴───> [Step 6: Cross-Site Inquiry Comparison]
                │
                └───> [Step 8: Contradictory Evidence & Negative Case Analysis]
                                        │
                                        └───> [Step 9: Methodological Limitations]
```

### Step 1: Data Familiarization & De-Identification
* Transcribe raw notes within 5 business days.
* Scrub all institutional names, classmate names, and identifiable locations.
* Read transcripts iteratively to understand the holistic participant context before tagging.

### Step 2: Systematic Open Coding
* Generate granular, descriptive codes for meaningful units of text (phrases, gestures, quotes).
* Maintain an open code registry with clear code descriptions and source pointers.

### Step 3: Pattern Identification & Code Clustering
* Collate related codes into conceptual clusters representing recurring behavioral patterns.
* Track code occurrence frequency across participants.

### Step 4: Candidate Theme Formulation
* Elevate clusters into candidate themes that provide explanatory insight into how learners collaborate.
* Test whether candidate themes address our core research questions.

### Step 5: Cross-Participant Matrix Analysis
* Tabulate candidate themes across all participant profiles (`P-01` to `P-07`).
* Compare behaviors between frequent group users vs. novice users, and residential vs. commuter students.

### Step 6: Cross-Site Observational Comparison
* Compare behavioral notes across the three contextual inquiry environments (`OBS-01` Library, `OBS-02` Lounge, `OBS-03` Cafeteria).
* Identify how spatial and acoustic constraints alter collaborative focus.

### Step 7: Triangulation Synthesis
* Synthesize findings across all three data streams (Observations + Interviews + Survey).
* A finding is classified as `VALIDATED` only when supported by at least two distinct data sources.

### Step 8: Contradictory Evidence & Negative Case Analysis
* Explicitly search for and document "negative cases" (e.g., participants who thrive in informal chat groups without issues).
* Refine or bound findings based on why certain users do not experience hypothesized friction.

### Step 9: Methodological Limitations & Bias Assessment
* Document sample constraints, geographic biases, and researcher confirmation bias checks.

## 3. Candidate Initial Codebook

These candidate codes represent analytical starting points mapped to our research inquiries. They will be applied, expanded, or discarded during actual coding:

| Candidate Code | Conceptual Definition | Mapped RQ | Example Observed Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CODE-DISC-COLD` | Social friction or hesitation in approaching unfamiliar peers to study. | `RQ-01` | "I didn't want to look desperate asking in the class chat." |
| `CODE-DISC-FILTER` | Inability to evaluate syllabus overlap or commitment level before meeting. | `RQ-01`, `RQ-06` | "We met up and realized he was 3 weeks behind on the problem sets." |
| `CODE-SCHED-FATIGUE` | Cognitive burnout caused by protracted back-and-forth messaging to find a free time slot. | `RQ-03` | "It took 30 messages just to agree on Tuesday at 4pm." |
| `CODE-STYLE-MISMATCH` | Friction caused by conflicting study modes (e.g., quiet focus vs. active socializing). | `RQ-04` | "I came to do practice exams, but they wanted to chat about the professor." |
| `CODE-MOMENTUM-DROP` | Gradual disengagement and dissolution of a study group due to lack of cadence or structure. | `RQ-05` | "After the first midterm, nobody sent a message, so it just died." |
| `CODE-ROLE-ABSENCE` | Lack of an identified host, agenda-setter, or facilitator to keep the group accountable. | `RQ-02`, `RQ-05` | "Nobody took charge, so we just stared at each other for an hour." |
| `CODE-TOOL-BURDEN` | Fragmentation caused by juggling multiple disconnected apps (WhatsApp, Drive, LMS). | `RQ-07` | "The zoom link was in Discord, the PDF was in email, and the time was on WhatsApp." |
| `CODE-TRUST-TRANSPARENCY` | Willingness to join a group when expectations, rules, and compatibility factors are explicit. | `RQ-08` | "I'd join if I knew exactly what they plan to cover and how they run meetings." |

## 5. Formal Distinctions in Reporting

To ensure transparency and intellectual honesty, reports in subsequent modules must strictly distinguish between:
* **Predefined Research Questions (`RQ-XXX`):** The questions we set out to answer.
* **Initial Analytical Codes (`CODE-XXX`):** Conceptual labels applied to raw transcripts and field notes.
* **Emerging Thematic Patterns:** Groupings of codes showing behavioral consistency.
* **Verified Empirical Findings:** Triangulated insights grounded in identifiable evidence (`OBS`, `INT`, `SUR`).
* **Validated User Needs (`NEED-XXX`):** Product requirements directly derived from verified empirical findings.
* **Unsupported Working Assumptions:** Initial hypotheses that field evidence failed to confirm.
