---

## 1. Contextual Inquiry Objectives

The contextual inquiry investigates how learners navigate physical, digital, and social workflows while studying individually and collaboratively. The objective is to observe real behavior *in situ*, identifying frictions, unspoken workarounds, and environmental constraints that participants often forget to mention in formal interviews.

### Core Observational Questions:
1. What digital tools and physical artifacts do students juggle simultaneously during group sessions?
2. How do groups negotiate transitions between silent focus, vocal problem-solving, and off-task socialization?
3. How do environmental factors (noise, seating layout, power outlets, Wi-Fi stability) shape study effectiveness?
4. What visible gestures, conversational cues, or digital alerts signal coordination breakdowns?

## 2. Observational Field Sites & Selection Criteria

Field observations will occur across three distinct campus environments characterized by varying social rules, acoustic levels, and spatial layouts:

| Site Identifier | Field Environment | Spatial & Social Characteristics | Primary Observational Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OBS-01** | **University Central Library** *(Quiet & Collaborative Study Floors)* | High acoustic regulation in quiet zones; zoned group tables in collaborative annexes; fixed power terminals; high density of laptops and reference materials. | Transitions between solitary focus and whispered peer coordination; tab-switching between course portals and messaging apps; laptop posture and focus duration. |
| **OBS-02** | **Campus Lounges & Department Study Commons** | Moderate noise; flexible modular furniture (armchairs, movable whiteboards, shared tables); high foot traffic; spontaneous drop-ins. | Impromptu group formation; whiteboard problem-solving; physical proximity negotiation; arrival and departure coordination. |
| **OBS-03** | **Campus Cafeterias & Student Union Common Areas** | High ambient noise; food/beverage consumption; frequent interruptions; limited power outlets; varied lighting; non-academic background chatter. | How groups maintain focus amidst loud distractions; vocal volume modulation; mobile phone glancing frequency; session fatigue and abandonment. |

## 3. Five Behavioral Observation Dimensions

Observers must structure their qualitative field recordings across five rigorous behavioral dimensions:

### Dimension 1: Tooling & Media Switching
* Which applications and physical tools are simultaneously open? (e.g., PDF reader, IDE, WhatsApp web, Canvas/LMS, notebook, iPad/stylus).
* How frequently do learners switch between study material and communication channels?
* Are study links, zoom links, or files passed via messy chat threads, email, or airdrop?

### Dimension 2: Coordination & Scheduling Gestures
* How are start times, break times, and session conclusions negotiated? (e.g., verbal cues, checking wristwatches/phones, setting timers).
* Do group members refer to digital calendars or negotiate ad-hoc ("Are you free tomorrow afternoon?")?
* What happens when a member arrives late or leaves early?

### Dimension 3: Actual Study Modes & Temporal Rhythms
* **Silent Co-Study:** Members sit together working on individual tasks without conversation.
* **Collaborative Problem-Solving:** Active side-by-side or shared-screen debugging, math derivations, or code walkthroughs.
* **Discussion / Socratic Debate:** Vocal conceptual exploration, questioning, and slide deck reviews.
* **Off-Task Socialization:** Drift into social media, casual conversations, or unrelated video browsing.
* *Record duration and frequency of mode transitions.*

### Dimension 4: Group Roles & Social Dynamics
* Does an informal facilitator or timekeeper emerge, or is leadership decentralized?
* Is participation egalitarian, or do dominant members monopolize the whiteboard/discussion?
* How are quieter members integrated or neglected?

### Dimension 5: Environmental & Physical Constraints
* How do physical seating arrangements (round table vs. linear bench vs. soft sofas) influence eye contact and screen sharing?
* Are learners constrained by access to power outlets, Wi-Fi blind spots, or ambient glare?
* How does ambient noise (cutlery, background music, passing students) disrupt train of thought?

## 4. Observer Instructions & Ethical Field Protocol

1. **Non-Intrusive Presence:** The observer sits at a natural distance in public study zones without looming, interfering, or interrupting active student sessions.
2. **Strict Anonymity:** Observers must never write down student names, read private text messages over shoulders, or log student ID cards.
3. **No Photography of Faces:** Photographs of room layout, furniture arrangements, or whiteboard diagrams are permitted only when no faces are visible.
4. **Immediate De-Briefing:** Observers must expand raw handwritten or shorthand digital notes into standardized observation records within 4 hours of leaving the field site.

## 6. Post-Observation Reflection Guide

Following each observation session, the researcher must complete an analytical reflection addressing:
1. What was the most surprising or counter-intuitive behavior observed?
2. Where did students experience the highest visible friction or cognitive load?
3. How did the observed behaviors support or challenge our working problem hypothesis?
4. What specific questions should be added or probed in upcoming participant interviews?
