## 1. Research Objectives & Grounding

The primary goal of the empirical research phase (Module 02) is to investigate the real-world behaviors, motivations, social dynamics, and friction points experienced by university students and independent learners when forming, coordinating, and sustaining peer study groups.

Specific research inquiries:
1. **RQ-01:** How do learners currently search for, evaluate, and assemble study groups?
2. **RQ-02:** How do learners initiate, structure, and organize new study groups?
3. **RQ-03:** What are the root causes of scheduling friction and meeting drop-offs?
4. **RQ-04:** How do differing study styles (silent vs. collaborative vs. discussion) affect group cohesion and success?
5. **RQ-05:** Why do informal study groups lose momentum and dissolve after 1–2 sessions?
6. **RQ-06:** What specific information (syllabus, style, schedule, expectations) do learners require *before* requesting to join a group?
7. **RQ-07:** How do learners currently navigate communication breakdowns across tools like WhatsApp, Discord, and campus forums?
8. **RQ-08:** What design mechanisms (transparency, rule explanations, role clarity) would establish user trust in a compatibility-matching platform?

## 3. Method 1: Contextual Inquiry Protocol

### 3.1 Observational Field Sites
Field observations will be conducted in three distinct natural study environments where student collaboration naturally occurs:
1. **University Central Library (Quiet & Collaborative Zones):** Observing how students transition between independent study and ad-hoc peer interaction; physical artifact arrangements; laptop usage.
2. **Dormitory / Campus Study Lounges:** Observing informal group formation, ambient distraction levels, and impromptu session coordination.
3. **Campus Cafeterias / Student Common Areas:** Observing how students coordinate across background noise, manage group seating, and handle schedule interruptions.

### 3.2 Observation Focus & Rubric
Observers will document field notes across five behavioral dimensions:
* **Tooling & Media Switching:** How often do learners switch between communication apps (WhatsApp/Discord), calendar tools, PDFs, and note-taking apps?
* **Coordination Gestures:** How are meeting times, agendas, and breaks negotiated?
* **Study Modes:** What proportion of time is spent in silent co-study vs. active verbal problem-solving vs. social distraction?
* **Group Dynamics:** Are roles (host, timekeeper, note-taker) apparent, or is the session unstructured?
* **Physical & Environmental Constraints:** How do power outlets, screen sharing, ambient noise, and seating arrangements constrain the study session?

## 4. Method 2: Semi-Structured Interview Protocol

### 4.1 Sample Criteria & Participant Quota
* **Core Qualitative Sample:** 5 to 7 enrolled university students (`P-01` through `P-07`).
* **Participant Diversity:** Balanced representation across STEM, humanities, and business fields; mix of commuter students (>30m travel) and on-campus residential students; frequent group study users vs. novices.
* **Supplementary Sample:** Autonomous independent learners may be included as a separate supplementary cohort, but must never replace the required core student sample.
* **Session Duration:** 25–35 minutes one-on-one sessions, conducted in-person on campus or via private audio call.
* **Operational Guide:** See [`docs/research/INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md`](research/INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md) and [`docs/research/RECRUITMENT_AND_SAMPLING.md`](research/RECRUITMENT_AND_SAMPLING.md).

### 4.2 Interview Guide & Question Script

The interview follows an open-ended, non-leading progression designed to elicit authentic past experiences:

#### Phase 1: Warm-Up & Learning Context (5 mins)
1. "Could you briefly share what subjects or topics you are currently focusing on, and how you typically study for them?"
2. "Do you usually study alone, in pairs, or in larger groups? What drives that preference?"

#### Phase 2: Past Group Formation Experiences (10 mins)
3. "Think back to the last time you tried to form or join a study group. How did that group come together?"
4. "What worked well during that process, and where did you experience friction or awkwardness?"
5. "Have you ever joined a study group that didn't meet your expectations or fell apart quickly? What happened?"

#### Phase 3: Scheduling, Coordination & Habits (10 mins)
6. "How do you and your study partners typically decide when and where to meet?"
7. "How do you handle differences in study habits—for example, if one person wants quiet focus and another wants to talk through every question?"
8. "What tools do you currently use to coordinate (chat apps, shared docs, calendars), and what frustrates you most about them?"

#### Phase 4: Envisioning Ideal Peer Learning (5 mins)
9. "If you could design an ideal way to connect with study partners who match your exact pace and schedule, what would that look like?"
10. "What information about a group would you need to see before deciding to invest your time with them?"

## 5. Method 3: Exploratory Survey Instrument

The survey validates qualitative patterns identified in interviews across a broader sample.

### Survey Questionnaire Structure:
* **Section 1: Academic Profile & Study Habits**
  * Q1: Primary field of study / learning track.
  * Q2: Average hours spent in group study per week (0, 1–3, 4–6, 7+).
  * Q3: Preferred study style (Single-choice & Multi-choice rating: Silent Co-Study, Problem-Solving, Discussion, Peer Teaching, Exam Drills).
* **Section 2: Group Formation & Coordination Pain Points (5-Point Likert Scale: 1 = Never, 5 = Constant)**
  * Q4: "Finding peers taking the exact same courses or learning topics."
  * Q5: "Finding meeting times that align across different schedules."
  * Q6: "Dealing with incompatible study habits or divergent commitment levels."
  * Q7: "Groups fizzling out after one or two meetings due to lack of structure."
* **Section 3: Feature Prioritization & Compatibility Preferences**
  * Q8: Rank the importance of: Schedule Overlap, Study Style Compatibility, Group Size Cap, Explicit Agenda, Physical vs. Online Format.

## 6. Summative Usability Evaluation Architecture (Module 09)

To ensure methodological continuity, the framework for the later summative usability evaluation is established now.

### 6.1 Clear Distinction of Participant Cohorts
* **Module 02 Qualitative Sample:** 5–7 participants for exploratory interviews and field contextual inquiries (identifying needs and problem validation).
* **Module 09 Summative Sample:** **18 distinct participants** for formal usability benchmarking against standardized metrics (SUS, SEQ, task completion, time-on-task).

### 6.2 Candidate Usability Benchmark Tasks

| Task ID | Benchmark Scenario & Objective | Primary Success Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **T01** | **Discover a Study Circle:** As a learner studying "Algorithms", discover and select a circle with evening availability and collaborative problem-solving style. | Successfully locates and opens the matching circle profile within 90 seconds. |
| **T02** | **Compare Group Profiles:** Inspect two candidate circles, review their compatibility score breakdown, and identify which group has the highest schedule overlap. | Correctly identifies and articulates the compatibility justification using the breakdown modal. |
| **T03** | **Join / Request Membership:** Submit an application to join a moderated circle, including a short introductory note describing your learning objectives. | Successfully completes submission; system confirms pending request on dashboard. |
| **T04** | **Create Circle & Schedule Session:** As a facilitator, create a new circle ("Web Systems") with a 5-member cap, and schedule the first session with an agenda. | Circle and session are successfully created; appear on circle profile and personal dashboard. |
| **T05** | **Manage Group & Session RSVP:** View upcoming session on personal dashboard, confirm RSVP attendance, and view participant list. | RSVP status updates to "Attending"; counter reflects participation accurately. |

### 6.3 Environmental Contexts Matrix
To test resilience across real-world student environments, testing will vary across three dimensions:

1. **Hardware Modality:**
   * Context A: Desktop / Laptop display (1920 &times; 1080) with mouse and keyboard.
   * Context B: Mobile smartphone viewport (375 &times; 812) with touch interactions.
2. **Ambient Environment:**
   * Context 1: Quiet setting (library / private study room).
   * Context 2: Moderately distracting setting (ambient student cafeteria noise).
3. **Temporal Constraint:**
   * Condition X: Standard unconstrained exploratory pace.
   * Condition Y: Time-sensitive scenario (e.g., "Find a study group before your next class in 3 minutes").
