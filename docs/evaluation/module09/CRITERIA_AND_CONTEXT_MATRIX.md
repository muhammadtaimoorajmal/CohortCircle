# Participant Selection Criteria & Context-of-Use Matrix

> **Document Purpose:** Detailed sampling specifications, participant eligibility criteria, and balanced context-of-use allocation matrix for the Module 09 summative evaluation.  
> **Target Sample:** $N = 18$ unique participants (`M09-P01` through `M09-P18`).  
> **Lifecycle Reference:** Module 09 Governance & Sampling Design.

---

## 1. Participant Selection Criteria (Experience Groups)

To ensure the evaluation represents the broader student and adult learner population, participants are stratified across three distinct levels of peer collaborative study experience:

### Group A: Frequent Group-Study Users ($n = 6$)
* **Definition:** Learners who actively and routinely participate in group study sessions for **&ge; 4 hours per week**.
* **Key Characteristics:**
  * Highly accustomed to collaborative problem-solving, peer review, and group scheduling.
  * Often experience friction with existing ad-hoc tools (WhatsApp, Discord, Google Calendar).
  * High expectations for scheduling agility, agenda structures, and roster accountability.
* **Assigned Participants:** `M09-P01`, `M09-P02`, `M09-P03`, `M09-P04`, `M09-P05`, `M09-P06`.

### Group B: Occasional Group-Study Users ($n = 6$)
* **Definition:** Learners who study with peers periodically (**1 to 3 hours per week**), primarily concentrated around project milestones, laboratory assignments, or midterm/final examination periods.
* **Key Characteristics:**
  * Balance solo study habits with occasional need for peer clarification or accountability.
  * Require low-overhead discovery; sensitive to complex onboarding or excessive coordination.
  * Value clear study style alignment (e.g., quiet co-study vs. active debate).
* **Assigned Participants:** `M09-P07`, `M09-P08`, `M09-P09`, `M09-P10`, `M09-P11`, `M09-P12`.

### Group C: Novice / Limited Group-Study Users ($n = 6$)
* **Definition:** Learners who engage in group study for **< 1 hour per week** or have never joined a formal study cohort.
* **Key Characteristics:**
  * Predominantly independent solo studiers who may find reaching out to peers intimidating.
  * Highest sensitivity to psychological safety, clear cohort norms, and transparent compatibility explanations.
  * Need high discoverability, intuitive terminology, and zero prerequisite technical setup.
* **Assigned Participants:** `M09-P13`, `M09-P14`, `M09-P15`, `M09-P16`, `M09-P17`, `M09-P18`.

## 2. Contexts-of-Use Criteria

In modern university environments, students access collaborative tools across varied physical settings and personal devices. The study incorporates two primary context dimensions:

### 2.1 Device Context
* **Laptop / Desktop ($n = 9$):**
  * Hardware: Windows, macOS, or Linux laptop/desktop display.
  * Viewport Resolution: &ge; 1024px width (standard widescreen layouts, sticky sidebar, table views).
  * Input Modality: Physical keyboard and mouse or precision trackpad.
* **Mobile Phone ($n = 9$):**
  * Hardware: iOS or Android modern smartphone.
  * Viewport Resolution: 360px–430px width (compact mobile viewports, collapsed bottom navigation bar, stacked touch grids).
  * Input Modality: Multi-touch gestures, virtual keyboard, minimum 44 &times; 44 CSS px touch targets.

### 2.2 Environmental Noise & Distraction Context
* **Quiet Study Environment ($n = 9$):**
  * Setting: Private dormitory room, quiet home workspace, or dedicated silent floor of a university library.
  * Conditions: Ambient noise &le; 45 dB; zero external interruptions; high focus.
* **Mildly Distracting Environment ($n = 9$):**
  * Setting: Campus student center, university cafeteria, coffee shop, or active dormitory common lounge.
  * Conditions: Ambient background noise 60–75 dB (conversational chatter, music, peripheral movement); realistic mobile cognitive load.
